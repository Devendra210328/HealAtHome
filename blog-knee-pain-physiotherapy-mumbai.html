Common Causes of Knee Pain in Mumbai

Understanding the root cause of your knee pain is the first step toward effective treatment. A qualified knee pain doctor in Mumbai or physiotherapist can help diagnose the exact condition, but here are the most common causes we treat:

1. Osteoarthritis (OA) of the Knee

Osteoarthritis is the leading cause of knee pain in adults over 45 in Mumbai. The cartilage that cushions the knee joint gradually wears down, causing bone-on-bone friction, stiffness, swelling, and pain that worsens with activity. Mumbai's humid climate can aggravate joint stiffness, making mornings particularly difficult. Knee pain treatment in Mumbai for arthritis focuses on strengthening the surrounding muscles, improving joint mobility, and managing inflammation through targeted physiotherapy exercises.

2. ACL and PCL Ligament Injuries

The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) and posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) are critical stabilizers of the knee joint. ACL tears are extremely common among cricket players, football enthusiasts, and weekend athletes across Mumbai's maidans and sports clubs. A sudden twist, pivot, or landing awkwardly can rupture these ligaments, causing severe pain, swelling, and knee instability. ACL injury physiotherapy in Mumbai is essential both before and after surgical reconstruction to restore full knee function.

3. Meniscal Tear

The meniscus is a C-shaped piece of cartilage that acts as a shock absorber between the thighbone and shinbone. Tears can occur from twisting movements during sports or from degenerative wear in older adults. Symptoms include knee locking, catching sensations, swelling, and difficulty straightening the leg fully. Many meniscal tears respond well to physiotherapy without requiring surgery.

4. Patellofemoral Syndrome (Runner's Knee)

Runner's knee causes a dull, aching pain around or behind the kneecap. It is especially common among Mumbai's growing community of marathon runners and joggers who train along Marine Drive, Bandra-Worli Sea Link promenades, and the Powai Lake track. Overuse, flat feet, weak quadriceps, or tight hamstrings can contribute to this condition. Physiotherapy addresses the muscular imbalances and biomechanical issues that cause runner's knee.

5. Post-Surgical Knee Pain (Knee Replacement Recovery)

Total knee replacement (TKR) surgery is increasingly common in Mumbai, with many hospitals in areas like Andheri, Dadar, and South Mumbai performing hundreds of procedures each year. Post-surgical rehabilitation through knee replacement physiotherapy in Mumbai is absolutely critical for a successful recovery. Without proper physiotherapy, scar tissue builds up, muscles atrophy, and the new joint never achieves its full range of motion.

6. Tendonitis (Patellar Tendonitis / Jumper's Knee)

Inflammation of the patellar tendon causes pain just below the kneecap, especially during jumping, climbing stairs, or squatting. Common in athletes and active individuals, this condition responds well to eccentric strengthening exercises and progressive loading under physiotherapy guidance.

Did you know? According to the Indian Journal of Orthopaedics, nearly 15% of adults in urban India suffer from osteoarthritis of the knee, with the prevalence rising to over 30% in those above 65 years of age. Early physiotherapy intervention can delay the progression of arthritis by strengthening the muscles that support and protect the knee joint.

When Should You See a Physiotherapist for Knee Pain?

Many people in Mumbai ignore knee pain, assuming it will go away on its own. While minor muscle soreness may resolve with rest, you should consult a physiotherapist for knee pain in Mumbai if you experience any of the following:

  • Persistent pain — Knee pain lasting more than 1-2 weeks that does not improve with rest and ice
  • Swelling or inflammation — Visible swelling around the knee joint that does not subside within a few days
  • Instability — A feeling that your knee is giving way or buckling when you walk or climb stairs
  • Locking or catching — The knee gets stuck in one position or you feel a catching sensation during movement
  • Reduced range of motion — Difficulty fully bending or straightening the knee
  • Pain during daily activities — Pain while walking, climbing stairs, sitting cross-legged, or getting up from a chair
  • Post-injury pain — Knee pain following a sports injury, fall, or accident
  • Post-surgical recovery — After knee arthroscopy, ACL reconstruction, or knee replacement surgery
  • Night pain — Knee pain that wakes you up at night or disrupts your sleep

Early intervention matters: Research published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine shows that patients who begin physiotherapy within 2 weeks of knee injury onset recover 40% faster than those who delay treatment. Do not wait for the pain to become unbearable before seeking help.

Best Physiotherapy Exercises for Knee Pain at Home

Our physiotherapist in Mumbai recommends these evidence-based exercises for managing knee pain at home. These exercises strengthen the muscles around the knee, improve stability, and reduce pain. Always consult a professional before starting, especially after surgery or a ligament injury.

1. Wall Squats (Wall Sits)

Stand with your back flat against a wall, feet shoulder-width apart and about 18 inches from the wall. Slowly slide down the wall until your thighs are parallel to the floor (or as far as comfortable without pain). Hold for 10-15 seconds, then slowly slide back up. Wall squats are excellent for strengthening the quadriceps — the primary muscle group that supports and stabilizes the knee joint — without placing excessive stress on the joint itself.

3 sets of 10 reps | Hold 10-15 seconds each

2. Straight Leg Raises (SLR)

Lie on your back with one leg bent and foot flat on the floor. Keep the other leg straight. Tighten the quadriceps of your straight leg and slowly lift it to the height of the opposite knee (about 12 inches off the ground). Hold for 3-5 seconds, then slowly lower. Straight leg raises are one of the safest and most effective exercises for knee rehabilitation. They strengthen the quadriceps without bending the knee, making them ideal for post-surgery and acute knee pain conditions.

3 sets of 15 reps each leg

3. Hamstring Curls (Standing)

Stand holding a chair or wall for balance. Slowly bend one knee, bringing your heel towards your buttocks. Hold for 3-5 seconds, then slowly lower. For added resistance, you can use an ankle weight (1-2 kg). Hamstring curls strengthen the back of the thigh, which works together with the quadriceps to provide balanced knee support. Weak hamstrings are a major risk factor for ACL injuries.

3 sets of 12 reps each leg

4. Calf Raises

Stand with feet hip-width apart, holding a chair for balance if needed. Slowly rise onto your toes, lifting your heels as high as possible. Hold at the top for 2-3 seconds, then slowly lower. Calf raises strengthen the gastrocnemius and soleus muscles, which play an important role in knee stability and shock absorption during walking and running. Strong calves reduce the load on the knee joint.

3 sets of 15 reps

5. Step-Ups

Stand facing a sturdy step or low platform (6-8 inches high to start). Place your affected foot on the step and slowly step up, straightening your knee fully. Slowly lower back down, controlling the movement. Step-ups are a functional exercise that mimics daily activities like climbing stairs — which is one of the most common complaints among knee pain patients in Mumbai. This exercise builds quadriceps and hip strength simultaneously.

3 sets of 10 reps each leg

Important safety note: Stop any exercise immediately if you experience sharp pain, increased swelling, or a popping sensation. These exercises are general guidelines. Post Knee Replacement Rehabilitation Timeline

Knee replacement physiotherapy in Mumbai follows a structured, progressive rehabilitation protocol. Recovery timelines can vary based on your age, fitness level, and whether it was a partial or total knee replacement. Here is the general rehabilitation roadmap our physiotherapists follow:

Week 1-2: Acute Recovery Phase

Physiotherapy begins within 24-48 hours after surgery, often while still in the hospital. The focus during this phase is on pain management, reducing swelling with ice and elevation, and starting gentle range-of-motion exercises. Ankle pumps, static quadriceps contractions, and assisted knee bending are initiated. The goal is to achieve 70-90 degrees of knee flexion by the end of week 2. Daily home physiotherapy sessions are recommended during this critical phase.

Week 3-6: Progressive Strengthening Phase

As pain and swelling decrease, the focus shifts to building strength and increasing range of motion. Exercises progress to straight leg raises, mini squats, stationary cycling, and gentle resistance training. Walking distance is gradually increased with a walker or cane. The target is 100-110 degrees of knee flexion by week 6. Sessions reduce to 3-4 times per week.

Week 6-12: Functional Recovery Phase

This is when significant progress occurs. Exercises advance to step-ups, balance training, stair climbing, and progressive resistance exercises. Most patients can walk without a walking aid by week 8-10. The goal is 115-125 degrees of flexion and the ability to perform daily activities independently. Sessions taper to 2-3 times per week.

Week 12-24: Return to Activity Phase

The final phase focuses on returning to your normal lifestyle and recreational activities. Advanced strengthening, proprioception training, and endurance exercises are prescribed. By 6 months, most patients achieve near-full function with 120-130 degrees of flexion. Our physiotherapist provides a long-term maintenance exercise program to protect the knee joint for years to come.

Success rate: Patients who complete their full course of post-knee replacement physiotherapy report 85-95% satisfaction with their surgical outcome. Those who skip or cut short their rehabilitation often struggle with stiffness, weakness, and limited function for months or even years after surgery.

ACL / PCL Injury Recovery with Physiotherapy

ACL injury physiotherapy in Mumbai is essential whether you undergo surgical reconstruction or opt for conservative (non-surgical) management. The ACL does not heal on its own, so rehabilitation focuses on building compensatory strength and stability in the surrounding muscles.

Pre-Surgery Rehabilitation (Prehab)

If ACL reconstruction surgery is planned, 2-4 weeks of pre-surgery physiotherapy (prehab) significantly improves surgical outcomes. Prehab focuses on reducing swelling, restoring full knee extension, and strengthening the quadriceps and hamstrings. Research shows that patients who complete prehab recover faster and achieve better functional outcomes after surgery.

Post-ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation

ACL recovery is a marathon, not a sprint. The typical rehabilitation timeline spans 6-9 months:

  • Weeks 1-2: Pain and swelling management, gentle range-of-motion exercises, patellar mobilization, and quadriceps activation
  • Weeks 2-6: Progressive range-of-motion exercises targeting full extension and 120+ degrees of flexion, stationary cycling, and closed-chain strengthening
  • Weeks 6-12: Progressive strengthening of quadriceps, hamstrings, and hip muscles, balance and proprioception training, pool-based exercises
  • Months 3-6: Advanced strengthening, jogging progression, agility drills, and sport-specific exercises
  • Months 6-9: Return-to-sport testing, plyometric training, and full sports clearance upon meeting strength and functional criteria

Conservative (Non-Surgical) ACL Management

Not all ACL injuries require surgery. Partial ACL tears, or complete tears in patients who do not participate in pivoting sports, can often be managed successfully with intensive physiotherapy. The focus is on building exceptional quadriceps and hamstring strength to compensate for the absent ligament, along with proprioception and neuromuscular training to prevent re-injury.

PCL Injury Rehabilitation

PCL injuries are less common but can occur from direct impact to the front of the knee (common in road accidents in Mumbai) or hyperextension injuries. Most PCL tears are treated conservatively with physiotherapy, focusing on progressive quadriceps strengthening, which is the primary muscle that resists the backward sliding of the tibia that a PCL injury allows.
