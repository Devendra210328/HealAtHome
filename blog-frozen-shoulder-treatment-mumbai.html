What Is Frozen Shoulder (Adhesive Capsulitis)?

Frozen shoulder is a condition where the capsule surrounding the shoulder joint becomes thickened, tight, and inflamed. The shoulder capsule is a connective tissue envelope that holds the ball-and-socket joint together. When this capsule develops adhesions (bands of scar tissue), the joint stiffens progressively and becomes extremely painful to move.

The condition typically affects one shoulder at a time (though it can occur in both) and develops slowly over weeks to months. It is most common in adults aged 40 to 65, and women are affected more frequently than men. In India, the condition is also commonly referred to as periarthritis shoulder, especially among older adults.

Key Fact: Frozen shoulder affects approximately 2-5% of the general population. However, the prevalence rises to 10-20% in people with diabetes — making it a particularly important condition for Mumbai's large diabetic population to be aware of. Early frozen shoulder physiotherapy in Mumbai can prevent the condition from reaching its most severe stage.

The 3 Stages of Frozen Shoulder

Understanding the three distinct stages of frozen shoulder is crucial for effective treatment. Each stage has different symptoms, and adhesive capsulitis treatment in Mumbai is tailored accordingly. Here is a detailed breakdown:

Duration: 2 - 9 Months

Stage 1: Freezing (Painful Stage)

This is the initial stage where the shoulder pain gradually worsens over weeks to months. The pain is often worse at night, making it difficult to sleep on the affected side. Movement starts to become limited, but stiffness is not yet the primary complaint — pain is.

  • Gradual onset of diffuse shoulder pain, often around the outer arm
  • Pain worsens with sudden or unguarded movements
  • Night pain disrupts sleep, especially when lying on the affected side
  • Early loss of external rotation (difficulty reaching behind your back)
  • Pain may radiate down the upper arm towards the elbow

Physiotherapy focus: Pain management through gentle range-of-motion exercises, ice/heat therapy, and manual therapy. The goal is to minimize inflammation and prevent further stiffening.

Duration: 4 - 12 Months

Stage 2: Frozen (Stiff Stage)

During this stage, the pain may actually begin to decrease, but the shoulder becomes significantly stiffer. Daily activities become very difficult as the range of motion is severely restricted in all directions. This is the stage where most patients in Mumbai seek professional help for shoulder pain physiotherapy.

  • Pain reduces slightly compared to the freezing stage
  • Marked stiffness and restriction in all directions of shoulder movement
  • Difficulty raising the arm above shoulder level
  • Unable to reach behind the back (e.g., tucking in a shirt or fastening a bra)
  • Activities like driving, cooking, and dressing become challenging
  • Muscle wasting (atrophy) may occur around the shoulder due to disuse

Physiotherapy focus: Aggressive but controlled stretching, joint mobilization techniques (Maitland or Mulligan), and progressive range-of-motion exercises to break adhesions and restore mobility.

Duration: 6 - 24 Months

Stage 3: Thawing (Recovery Stage)

In this final stage, shoulder movement gradually begins to return. Pain continues to decrease, and the range of motion slowly improves. With dedicated frozen shoulder physiotherapy in Mumbai, this stage can be significantly shortened.

  • Gradual improvement in shoulder range of motion
  • Pain continues to diminish week by week
  • Strength begins to return as movement improves
  • Daily activities become progressively easier
  • Full recovery is possible with consistent physiotherapy and home exercises

Physiotherapy focus: Progressive strengthening, advanced stretching, functional exercises, and return to full daily activities and work.

Important: Without treatment, the entire frozen shoulder cycle from freezing to thawing can take 18 months to 3 years. With structured frozen shoulder treatment in Mumbai from a qualified physiotherapist, recovery can be accelerated to 3-6 months. The earlier you start treatment, the faster you recover.

Causes and Risk Factors for Frozen Shoulder

While the exact cause of frozen shoulder is not always clear, several factors significantly increase the risk of developing this condition. Understanding these risk factors is especially important for Mumbai residents:

1. Diabetes Mellitus

People with diabetes are 2-4 times more likely to develop frozen shoulder. High blood sugar levels cause changes in the collagen of the shoulder capsule, making it more prone to thickening and stiffening. Given that Mumbai has a very high prevalence of diabetes, this is a major risk factor for the city's population. If you have diabetes and notice shoulder stiffness, seek adhesive capsulitis treatment in Mumbai early.

2. Thyroid Disorders

Both hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) and hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid) are associated with frozen shoulder. Thyroid hormones influence tissue metabolism, and imbalances can affect the shoulder capsule's health. Regular thyroid check-ups are recommended if you develop unexplained shoulder stiffness.

3. Shoulder Injury or Trauma

A fracture, rotator cuff tear, or any injury to the shoulder can trigger the adhesive capsulitis process. The inflammation and subsequent immobilization after an injury create the perfect conditions for the shoulder capsule to stiffen. Prompt physiotherapy after any shoulder injury is essential to prevent frozen shoulder.

4. Post-Surgery Immobilization

Prolonged immobilization of the shoulder after surgery — whether shoulder surgery, heart surgery (via sternotomy), or breast surgery — is a well-known trigger. When the shoulder is kept still in a sling or brace for extended periods, the capsule can contract. Post-operative physiotherapy is critical to maintaining shoulder mobility.

5. Other Risk Factors

  • Age 40-65: The condition rarely occurs outside this age range
  • Women: More commonly affected than men (ratio approximately 60:40)
  • Cardiac conditions: Patients recovering from heart attack or cardiac surgery
  • Stroke: Paralysis or weakness on one side can lead to shoulder stiffening
  • Parkinson's disease: Reduced arm movement increases risk
  • Sedentary lifestyle: Lack of regular shoulder movement, common among desk workers in Mumbai's corporate hubs like BKC, Lower Parel, and Andheri

Why Physiotherapy Is the Best Treatment for Frozen Shoulder

Among all available treatment options for frozen shoulder, physiotherapy consistently ranks as the most effective non-invasive approach. Here is why shoulder pain physiotherapy in Mumbai should be your first line of treatment:

  • Addresses the root cause: Unlike painkillers that only mask symptoms, physiotherapy directly targets the stiffened capsule through stretching, mobilization, and manual therapy techniques that break down adhesions
  • Avoids surgery: Research published in the Journal of Shoulder and Elbow Surgery shows that over 90% of frozen shoulder cases resolve completely with physiotherapy alone, eliminating the need for surgical intervention
  • Customized treatment: A qualified physiotherapist tailors the treatment based on which stage of frozen shoulder you are in — gentle pain relief during the freezing stage, aggressive mobilization during the frozen stage, and strengthening during the thawing stage
  • Prevents recurrence: Physiotherapy strengthens the surrounding muscles and improves joint mechanics, reducing the risk of the condition returning
  • Safe for diabetics: Unlike corticosteroid injections that can spike blood sugar levels, physiotherapy is completely safe for diabetic patients — a crucial advantage given Mumbai's high diabetes prevalence
  • No side effects: Physiotherapy has zero side effects compared to long-term use of anti-inflammatory medications or painkillers
  • Faster return to daily life: Structured physiotherapy helps you regain the ability to perform daily tasks like dressing, cooking, driving, and working much faster

Clinical Evidence: A landmark study comparing physiotherapy with corticosteroid injections for frozen shoulder found that while injections provided faster short-term pain relief (first 6 weeks), physiotherapy patients had significantly better long-term outcomes at 6 and 12 months. The physiotherapy group had greater range of motion and lower recurrence rates.

5 Best Exercises for Frozen Shoulder Recovery

These exercises are recommended by our physiotherapist in Mumbai for frozen shoulder rehabilitation. Always start gently, never force through severe pain, and consult a professional before beginning any exercise program. Perform these exercises 2-3 times daily for best results.

1. Pendulum Stretch (Codman's Exercise)

Lean forward with your unaffected hand resting on a table for support. Let your affected arm hang freely. Gently swing the arm in small circles — 10 clockwise, 10 anti-clockwise. Then swing forward-and-back and side-to-side, 10 times each. As your shoulder loosens over days, gradually increase the diameter of the circles. This exercise uses gravity to gently distract the joint and reduce stiffness with minimal pain.

2. Towel Stretch (Internal Rotation Stretch)

Hold a towel behind your back with both hands — one hand reaching over the shoulder from above, the other reaching up from below. Use the top hand to gently pull the lower hand (affected arm) upward, stretching the shoulder. Hold for 15-20 seconds and release slowly. Repeat 5-8 times. This exercise specifically targets the internal rotation restriction that is a hallmark of frozen shoulder.

3. Finger Wall Walk (Flexion Exercise)

Stand facing a wall at arm's length. Place your fingertips of the affected arm on the wall at waist level. Slowly walk your fingers up the wall, raising your arm as high as you comfortably can. Hold the highest position for 10 seconds, then slowly walk your fingers back down. Repeat 8-10 times. Each day, try to reach a little higher. This exercise progressively improves forward flexion of the shoulder.

4. Cross-Body Stretch (Horizontal Adduction)

Sit or stand upright. Use your unaffected hand to lift your affected arm at the elbow and bring it across your body towards the opposite shoulder. Hold the stretch for 20-30 seconds, feeling a gentle pull in the back of the affected shoulder. Repeat 5-8 times. This stretch helps restore the ability to reach across your body — essential for activities like putting on a seatbelt or reaching into a cupboard.

5. External Rotation Stretch (with Stick or Doorframe)

Stand in a doorway with your affected arm's elbow bent at 90 degrees and pressed against your side. Place your forearm against the doorframe. Gently rotate your body away from the doorframe, keeping the elbow pinned to your side, until you feel a stretch in the front of the shoulder. Hold for 20-30 seconds and release. Repeat 5-8 times. External rotation is usually the most restricted movement in frozen shoulder, and this exercise directly addresses it.

Pro Tip from Our Physiotherapist: Apply a warm towel or hot water bag to your shoulder for 10-15 minutes before doing these exercises. Heat increases blood flow, relaxes the muscles, and makes stretching more effective. After exercising, apply an ice pack for 10 minutes to reduce any inflammation. This warm-before, cold-after approach significantly improves results for periarthritis shoulder treatment.
